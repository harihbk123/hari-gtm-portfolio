What does a GTM engineer do?

A GTM (go-to-market) engineer builds the systems behind pipeline: the data, automation and infrastructure that take a lead from first signal to booked meeting. That covers signal-based list building, Clay enrichment waterfalls, cold email infrastructure and deliverability, AI research agents, CRM hygiene and reporting, so a small team can do the work of a large SDR floor.

Do you work with startups and smaller budgets?

Yes. Budget is never my first filter. If the problem is interesting and you care about doing it properly, I'll find a scope that fits, often a smaller first build that pays for itself before we expand.

I trust the quality of my work, and a client who sees the system working matters more to me than the size of the first invoice.

Do you work with companies outside India?

Yes, most of my clients are outside India, mainly in the US, UK and Europe. Working from Bengaluru (IST), I overlap with European business hours and US mornings, and builds move forward overnight while US teams sleep.

Can you set up cold email infrastructure from scratch?

Yes, the full stack: domain selection, DNS (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), Google Workspace and Microsoft inboxes, warmup, sender rotation, capacity planning and daily blacklist monitoring. I've run fleets of 5,000+ inboxes across 400+ domains and built the tooling that provisions and heals them automatically.

Which GTM tools do you work with?

80+ tools. The core: Clay, Apollo, LeadMagic, AI Ark and TheirStack for data and signals; Smartlead, Instantly, EmailBison, Outreach, Lemlist and HeyReach for sending; Zapmail, Hypertide, InboxKit and GlockApps for infrastructure and deliverability; n8n, HubSpot, Salesforce and Attio for automation and RevOps; Claude Code, Claude, OpenAI and MCP for AI agents; and Vercel, Neon, Supabase, Railway and Cloudflare to ship custom tools.
